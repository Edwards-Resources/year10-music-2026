Lesson 4

Lesson 4: Texture across styles (listen and build)

Lesson 4 of 30, in Week 2 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can identify and describe texture in different styles.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Key word

Texture

The way layers of sound are combined in a piece of music.

Listen

Suggestions: Gregorian chant (search ‘Gregorian chant Kyrie’), or unaccompanied solo voice/instrument.

Key word

Monophonic

A single melodic line with no accompaniment.

Listen

Suggestions: a simple pop or hymn arrangement (e.g. a band playing melody over strummed/block chords), or a piano/vocal ballad.

Key word

Homophonic

A melody with chordal accompaniment.

Listen

Suggestions: a Bach fugue (e.g. ‘Little Fugue in G minor’), a round/canon (e.g. ‘Frère Jacques’ sung in canon), or a jazz combo with independent improvised lines.

Key word

Polyphonic

Two or more independent melodic lines combined.

What we are doing

  1. Read the texture definition and each texture type.
  2. Listen to the excerpt after each definition and check it matches.
  3. In groups, build a texture by adding one layer at a time.
  4. Describe how the texture changed as layers were added.

Your task

For each excerpt, name the texture type and explain how the layers combine to create it.

Before you leave

Which excerpt had the thickest texture, and why?
