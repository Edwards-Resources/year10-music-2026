Lesson 13: Exam feedback and reflection

Lesson 13 of 28, in Week 5 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can evaluate my own listening response and set a goal.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Read this

The gap between your band and the next one is written on the rubric, not guessed.

Every band above yours describes the same thing you did, taken one step further. Reading only your own band and the top band hides that step, so start by reading the whole ladder, not just the two ends of it.

How to read the gap

  • Find the rubric below and locate the criterion your teacher marked you against.
  • Read all five bands for that criterion, top to bottom, not just yours.
  • Find your band, then read the band directly above it.
  • Underline the one thing that band adds that your band's wording does not.
  • Check that against your own paper, honestly: did you actually do that, or use different words to sound like you did?

Working rule: the gap is almost never ‘write more’. It is one missing move that the band above adds and yours does not - naming something specific, connecting two things, or explaining an effect.

A worked example

Take the Analysis criterion in the rubric below. A C answer ‘describes how some elements are used, with limited analysis of how they combine’: each element gets its own sentence, reported on its own. A B answer ‘analyses how several elements are used and combined, mostly accurately’: the elements are linked to each other inside the one sentence, not just listed side by side. ‘The dynamics get louder. The texture thickens.’ is two C-band observations sitting next to each other. ‘The dynamics get louder as the texture thickens, and together that drives the music into the final chorus’ is the same two facts, joined the way B asks for.

That joining move is the same one Lesson 9 calls the effect. You met the response sentence first in Lesson 3 and in full in Lesson 9: name, how it is used, effect, evidence. The same four-part shape closes the gap on any of the four criteria below, not only Analysis.

AT3 Aural Skills: marking criteria

20 marks, 25% of the course. You sat this on Wednesday 19 August 2026 under exam conditions: Part A was short answer on several short excerpts, Part B was one extended response to a final excerpt. The marks: identification 5, analysis 5, terminology 4, extended response 6.

  • A17-20
  • B13-16
  • C9-12
  • D5-8
  • E0-4

  • Accurately identify the elements of music and the style/context of each excerpt (Part A)

    • AIdentifies elements and style/context accurately and comprehensively across all excerpts.
    • BIdentifies most elements and style/context accurately with minor gaps.
    • CIdentifies the main elements and style with some accuracy; some gaps or errors.
    • DIdentifies a few elements with limited accuracy.
    • EIdentifies elements with significant errors or omissions.

  • Analyse how the elements of music are manipulated and combined (Parts A and B)

    • AAnalyses precisely how elements are manipulated and combined to create effect.
    • BAnalyses how several elements are used and combined, mostly accurately.
    • CDescribes how some elements are used, with limited analysis of how they combine.
    • DMakes simple statements about elements with little analysis.
    • ELists elements with little or no analysis.

  • Use correct musical terminology and specific musical evidence

    • AUses correct terminology fluently and supports points with specific musical evidence.
    • BUses correct terminology and relevant evidence with minor lapses.
    • CUses some terminology and evidence; not always accurate or specific.
    • DUses limited terminology; evidence is vague or general.
    • EUses little or no accurate musical terminology.

  • Sustain a clear, well-structured extended response on how the elements create interest (Part B)

    • ASustains a clear, well-structured response linking several elements to interest, with strong evidence.
    • BDevelops a clear response linking several elements to interest, with relevant evidence.
    • CProvides a response covering some elements; structure or links to interest are uneven.
    • DProvides a brief or partly relevant response with limited links to interest.
    • EProvides a minimal or unclear response.
Check yourself: reading the gap
  1. When you compare your answer to a criterion, which bands should you read first? All five, top to bottom, not just your own band and the top one. The step you need is almost always the one directly above your own band, not the one at the very top.
  2. In the worked example above, what was the actual difference between a C and a B on the Analysis criterion? A C reports each element in its own sentence. A B links two elements together inside one sentence. Same underlying facts, joined rather than left side by side.
  3. Can this page tell you which band your own exam answer landed in? No - that only exists on your marked paper and your teacher's feedback. This page can hand back the process and the fixed facts about the task. The band you actually got, and the gap you actually need to close, is yours to bring from your own paper, not something a page can know in advance.
  4. If you do not have your marked paper, what can you redraft instead? Your Lesson 10 timed response, or a fresh one written against any excerpt cued on that page, or a fresh dot point marked against the criteria above. Either way you run the same process: find the band, read the one above it, close the gap.
  5. Which of the four criteria in the rubric above is worth the most marks, and which part of the exam is it in? The extended response criterion, worth 6, in Part B. The other three criteria (identification, analysis, terminology) make up the remaining 14, mostly earned in Part A.
  6. The exit asks for a listening goal. What makes one worth writing down? A goal that names the move you are missing, not an amount of work. “Listen harder” and “write more” cannot be checked and cannot be finished. “In every dot point this term, write the effect as well as the change” names one move, comes straight off the band above yours, and you can tell at a glance whether you did it.

What we are doing

  1. Read your marked response next to your teacher's feedback.
  2. Find your band on the rubric above, for each of the four criteria.
  3. Pick one criterion and read the band directly above yours (see the box above for how to find the gap).
  4. Re-draft one weak dot point from your exam to close that gap. Use Lesson 9’s worked example - the modelled dot point on “What a Wonderful World” - as your exemplar for the standard.
  5. Set a personal listening goal for the rest of the term.

Read this

You close the gap by rewriting one dot point, not by writing more of them.

Closing the gap is not a length problem. A longer version of the same C-band sentence is still a C-band sentence: more words, still describing elements one at a time instead of connecting them. The fix is the one move you underlined above, made inside the sentence you already have.

Working rule: rewrite the sentence, do not add a second one next to it. If your redraft needs a new sentence to carry the missing move, the move still is not inside your analysis - it is sitting beside it.

Your task

Re-draft one weak dot point from your exam to a higher standard. Find your band on the rubric above, read the band above it, and close the one gap between them by rewriting the sentence, not by adding to it. If you do not have your marked paper, use your Lesson 10 timed response instead, or work from one of the four excerpts cued on that page, which the last answer in the check links to, or write a fresh dot point answering ‘How are the elements of music used to create interest in this excerpt?’, mark it yourself against the Analysis criterion above, then re-draft that.

Before you leave

Write your listening goal for the rest of Term 3.
