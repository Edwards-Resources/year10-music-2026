Lesson 17: Film music listening

Lesson 17 of 28, in Week 7 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can identify how film music supports narrative.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Read this

Film music is not there to be noticed. It is there to tell you how to feel about what you are looking at.

Cut the same shot of someone walking down a corridor to three different pieces of music and you get three different scenes: a thriller, a comedy, a farewell. Nothing on screen changed. That is the whole job, and it means every choice in a film score is answerable to something happening in the story.

Three techniques with names

  • Leitmotif. A short musical idea attached to a character, a place or an idea, brought back whenever they are relevant, and often altered to show what has happened to them since. Borrowed from opera and standard in film ever since.
  • Underscore. Music running under dialogue or action, deliberately kept out of the way. If you consciously noticed it during the film, it was probably doing its job badly.
  • Mickey-mousing. Music matching the action moment for moment, a musical gesture per footstep or per pratfall. Named after the cartoons that made it famous. Obvious, and usually comic when used in live action.

Working rule: name the feature and the moment it serves. “The music is tense” is not analysis, because it describes your reaction rather than the music. “The strings move to a fast tremolo as the door opens, which turns an ordinary action into a threat” names what the composer did, when, and what it bought them. That is the same four-part sentence from Lesson 3, pointed at a screen.

Be careful with rules like “minor key means sad”. They hold often enough to be worth knowing and are broken constantly by good composers. Describe what you actually hear in this cue, not what the textbook says the key should mean.

What we are doing

  1. Meet the techniques: leitmotif, underscore, mickey-mousing.
  2. Listen to the cues and match musical features to on-screen meaning.
  3. Connect back to Term 1 program music and your Term 2 soundtrack task.
  4. Discuss how the elements signal emotion.

Your task

For each cue, link one musical feature to the emotion or action it supports.
