Lesson 8

Lesson 8: Structure

Lesson 8 of 30, in Week 3 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can identify the structure of a work by ear.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Key word

Structure

How the piece is put together — the order of its sections, like the shape of a story.

Listen

A pop song with a clear verse-chorus structure.

Key word

Verse-chorus

The pop-song structure: verses tell the story, the chorus is the hook that repeats.

Listen

A 12-bar blues.

Key word

12-bar blues (AAB)

A repeating 12-bar chord pattern, often with an AAB lyric pattern — the backbone of blues and early rock 'n' roll.

Listen

A classical work in ternary form or theme and variation.

Key word

Ternary / theme and variation

Ternary (ABA): a section, a contrasting middle, then a return to the start. Theme and variation: one melody, dressed up differently each time it repeats.

What we are doing

  1. Recap forms: verse-chorus, 12-bar blues, ternary (ABA), theme and variation.
  2. Listen and map the sections of each excerpt.
  3. Discuss what tells you a new section has started (a change in an element).
  4. Compare how the three structures are organised.

Your task

Map the structure of one excerpt by labelling its sections and noting what changes between them.

Before you leave

What element signalled a new section most clearly?
