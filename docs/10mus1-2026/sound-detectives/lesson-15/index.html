Lesson 15

Lesson 15: Jazz practical: 12-bar blues

Lesson 15 of 28, in Week 6 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can perform and improvise within a 12-bar blues.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Read this

Twelve bars, three chords, and everybody arrives back at the top together.

The 12-bar blues is a fixed pattern of twelve bars that repeats for the length of the song. It is the reason musicians who have never met can play together for ten minutes without discussing anything first: once everyone knows where they are in the twelve, the rest looks after itself. You heard this pattern run nine times without changing in Lesson 8.

The pattern

  • Bars 1 to 4 - I, the home chord.
  • Bars 5 and 6 - IV.
  • Bars 7 and 8 - back to I.
  • Bar 9 - V, the furthest the pattern ever gets from home.
  • Bar 10 - IV.
  • Bars 11 and 12 - home to I, then a turnaround that hands you back to bar 1.

The numbers work in any key. In A, the most common classroom blues key, I is A7, IV is D7 and V is E7. Learn the pattern by its numbers rather than its chord names and you can move it to any key you are asked for.

Whatever role you take

  • Chords - change on the right bar, not when it sounds like time. Count in fours the whole way through.
  • Bass - the root note of whichever chord is current, steady, no decoration needed.
  • Rhythm and drums - do not change with the chords. Your job is that the groove never wavers.
  • Melody and solos - use the blues scale, and aim to land somewhere sensible by bar 12.

Working rule: the form does not stop for your solo. The twelve bars keep turning underneath whether you play a note or not, so keep counting while you improvise. Coming back in at the wrong bar is the only real way to get lost in a blues.

Listen

12-bar blues backing track in A major, 100 bpm. A comfortable tempo for the class to play along with and improvise over.

What we are doing

  1. Learn the 12-bar blues chord pattern.
  2. Take a role: chords, bass, rhythm or melody.
  3. Play through the form as a class with the backing track.
  4. Try a short improvised solo using the blues scale.

Your task

Perform the 12-bar blues as an ensemble; everyone attempts at least a two-note improvised idea.

Before you leave

Did you keep your place in the form? What helped?
