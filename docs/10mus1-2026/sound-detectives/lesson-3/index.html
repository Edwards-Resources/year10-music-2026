Lesson 3

Lesson 3: Writing about music 1

Lesson 3 of 28, in Week 1 of Sound Detectives.

Learning intention

I can write a clear sentence about how an element is used.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Read this

Name it, say how it is used, say what it does, say where you heard it.

Writing about music is not writing about how the music made you feel. It is evidence. Every point you make has four parts, and a sentence missing one of them is usually the reason an answer scores lower than the student expected.

  • Name the element you are writing about.
  • How it is used - what you actually hear, in enough detail that the marker can hear it too.
  • Effect - what that does to the music or to the listener. This is the part people leave out.
  • Evidence - where in the music it happens.

You will meet this again in Lesson 9 with a fully worked example and a marked-out zero-mark answer. For now, get all four parts into every sentence you write, and check your partner's for the one they dropped.

What we are doing

  1. Learn the response sentence: element + how it is used + effect + evidence.
  2. Build a class response to a short excerpt together.
  3. Write one dot point on your own.
  4. Swap with a partner and check against the sentence frame.

Your task

Write one dot point about a short excerpt using the sentence frame, for at least two elements.

Before you leave

Underline the ‘effect’ part of your best sentence.

Slides and notes

  • Model the sentence on the board: element + how it is used + effect + musical evidence. Build one together before students write.
