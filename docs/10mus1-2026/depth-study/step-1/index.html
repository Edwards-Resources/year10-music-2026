Lesson 1: Choose your project

Step 1 of 5, worked on in Term 3, Weeks 9 and 10.

Learning intention

I can choose a depth study project I care about and can finish in the time I have.

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

Key word

Depth study
A self-directed music investigation in an area of personal interest. You choose the area, you choose the product, and you drive the work. This is the most independent unit of Year 10.

IdeasWhat you could do

Three focus areas, and a fourth option that combines two of them. Each row is the same project at three sizes, so find the one that matches where you actually are. You can also propose your own idea, as long as it fits a focus area.

  • Performing

    • Entry Prepare and perform one piece on your instrument, with short program notes.
    • Core Perform a set of two or three linked pieces, or record a multitrack cover of a song you love.
    • Extension Lead a small ensemble, or perform a demanding programme you have chosen and rehearsed yourself.

    You hand in a live or recorded performance, plus program notes or a rehearsal log.

  • Composing

    • Entry Write a short original piece or song section and explain the choices you made.
    • Core Write an original song and record a demo, or arrange an existing song in a new style.
    • Extension Compose to a brief: music for a film scene, a game, an artwork, or a produced track.

    You hand in a recording or demo, plus a score or lead sheet if your piece has one, and a short rationale.

  • Listening and Investigation

    • Entry A short presentation analysing how the elements are used in a track you already love.
    • Core A multimodal investigation of an artist, a genre or a soundtrack, with examples.
    • Extension A viva voce where you argue a case about a style and answer questions on it.

    You hand in a multimodal presentation or a viva voce, with musical examples you play.

  • Combination

    • Entry Compose a short piece and then perform it yourself.
    • Core Investigate a style, then perform or compose something in it.
    • Extension Research, compose and perform, and explain how each part fed the next.

    You hand in a product from each area you choose, plus a short piece of writing linking them.

Step by stepHow to choose well

  1. Interest. Pick something you actually care about. You are going to live with this for a term, and it shows in the final product when you do not.
  2. Skill. Pick something you can already do most of, and stretch the last part. A project that needs a skill you have not started learning is a project you will not finish.
  3. Time. You have eight to ten hours of class time and the weeks between. Halve your first idea if it does not fit that, and do the smaller thing properly.

Your task

Write down two projects you would genuinely like to do, one from each of two different focus areas. For each one, note what you would hand in and the single hardest part. Bring both to your proposal conference: choosing between two real options is much easier than inventing one on the spot.

Exit ticket

In one sentence: what do you want to make, and why that one?

Depth Study

Before Term 4: Choosing and proposing

The work that decides how the whole unit goes. You choose a project that you actually care about and can actually finish, then write the plan that gets it approved.

  1. 1Choose your projectI can choose a depth study project I care about and can finish in the time I have.
  2. 2Write your proposalI can write a proposal that sets out my goal, my product, my plan and how I will know it worked.

Term 4, Weeks 1 to 3: Developing it

The longest stretch of the unit. You build your product, get feedback twice, and keep a running record of your thinking so the write-up for your presentation is easy at the end.

  1. 3Develop itI can work steadily on my project, use feedback to improve it, and keep a process journal that shows my thinking.

Term 4, Weeks 3 to 4: Refining and finalising

You compare your work honestly against the rubric for your focus area, fix the highest-impact gaps first, and check your submission is actually ready before the due date.

  1. 4Refine and finaliseI can check my project against the rubric for my focus area, fix the gaps that matter most, and get it ready to submit.

Term 4, Weeks 4 to 5: Presenting it

You submit your product, present it to the class with a short spoken introduction, and then take your turn as an audience member for everyone else’s presentations.

  1. 5Present itI can introduce my project to the class, present it clearly, and give useful feedback as an audience member.
