Step 2

Step 2: Write your proposal

Step 2 of 2, worked on in Term 3, Week 10 and Term 4, Week 1.

Learning intention

I can write a proposal that sets out my goal, my product, my plan and how I will know it worked.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

What a proposal has to do

  1. Make the project real. A goal like "do something with jazz" cannot be started on Monday. "Learn and record two Miles Davis heads with a backing track" can.
  2. Make it checkable. If nobody can tell whether you have finished, you cannot tell either.
  3. Make it small enough. Your plan has to fit the weeks that actually exist, including the ones where you are away or something breaks.

Your proposal

Fill this in on screen, then talk it through with Mr Edwards to get it approved. What you type stays on this device, so finish it in one sitting or write it up somewhere safe.

  1. Focus area

    Performing, Composing, Listening and Investigation, or a combination?

  2. My goal

    What are you trying to do, make or find out? Write it so someone else could tell when you had done it.

  3. My product

    What exactly will you hand in or present? Name the thing: a recording, a demo and lead sheet, a viva voce, a multimodal presentation.

  4. My outcomes

    Which outcomes will your project give evidence for? They come from the focus area you chose, and they are listed on the unit page.

  5. My plan

    What will you have done by the end of each week, up to the due date in Week 4? Give each week one milestone you could tick off.

  6. What I need

    Instruments, software, recording gear, a practice room, and the parts where you will need help.

  7. Success criteria

    How will you know your project worked? Write three things a good version of it would have. We build these together from the marking guide.

Your answers stay on this device.

Key word

Success criteria

The agreed signs that show your project has met the standard. They are not the same as finishing it. A project can be handed in complete and still miss what it was aiming at, which is exactly what these are for.

Your task

Swap proposals with someone doing a different focus area. Read theirs and ask one question about the part you could not picture. The parts nobody can picture are the parts that go wrong in Week 3.

Before you leave

Which week of your plan is the one most likely to slip, and what will you do about it?
