Step 4

Step 4: Refine and finalise

Step 4 of 5, worked on in Term 4, Weeks 3 and 4.

Learning intention

I can check my project against the rubric for my focus area, fix the gaps that matter most, and get it ready to submit.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

How to self-assess without kidding yourself

  1. Read all five bands for a criterion before you decide where you sit, not just the top one. The middle bands describe most real projects.
  2. Be specific. "I'm okay at technique" is not a self-assessment; "my technique is secure in the first half but breaks down in the fast section" is.
  3. Fix the biggest gap first. A perfect ending on a project that is not finished scores worse than a finished project with a rough ending.

Performing rubric

Four criteria, 5 marks each, 20 marks total. Use this if your focus area is Performing.

  • A17-20
  • B13-16
  • C9-12
  • D5-8
  • E0-4

  • Technical control and accuracy

    • AHighly developed, fluent and secure technique throughout; negligible errors.
    • BStrong control; mostly fluent with minor inconsistencies.
    • CSound control; generally accurate with some errors.
    • DLimited control; frequent errors affect the performance.
    • EMinimal control; performance is insecure.

  • Expression and stylistic interpretation

    • ASophisticated, consistent expression and stylistic understanding.
    • BEffective expression and mostly appropriate style.
    • CSome expression and stylistic awareness; inconsistent.
    • DLimited expression or stylistic awareness.
    • ELittle or no expression or stylistic awareness.

  • Realisation of intention and repertoire choice

    • AAmbitious, well-chosen program fully realised as intended.
    • BSuitable program realised well.
    • CReasonable program partly realised.
    • DSimple program with limited realisation.
    • EProgram not realised.

  • Presentation and communication

    • AConfident, engaging presentation with a clear spoken introduction.
    • BClear presentation and introduction.
    • CAdequate presentation; introduction lacks detail.
    • DBasic presentation; little communication.
    • EMinimal presentation.

Composing rubric

Four criteria, 5 marks each, 20 marks total. Use this if your focus area is Composing.

  • A17-20
  • B13-16
  • C9-12
  • D5-8
  • E0-4

  • Manipulation and combination of the elements of music

    • AManipulates and combines elements with control and imagination throughout.
    • BManipulates and combines several elements effectively.
    • CUses some elements with limited combination.
    • DUses few elements with little control.
    • EMinimal control of the elements.

  • Stylistic understanding and musical expression

    • AStrong, consistent style and expressive intent.
    • BClear style and expression with minor lapses.
    • CSome stylistic understanding and expression.
    • DLimited stylistic understanding.
    • ELittle or no stylistic understanding.

  • Structure, development and realisation of intention

    • AWell-structured, developed work that fully realises the concept.
    • BClear structure and development; concept realised.
    • CBasic structure; concept partly realised.
    • DWeak structure; limited realisation.
    • ELittle structure or realisation.

  • Presentation and communication (demo and spoken introduction)

    • APolished recording or demo with a clear spoken introduction.
    • BGood-quality demo and clear introduction.
    • CAdequate demo; introduction lacks detail.
    • DBasic demo; little communication.
    • EMinimal demo or communication.

Listening and Investigation rubric

Four criteria, 5 marks each, 20 marks total. Use this if your focus area is Listening and Investigation.

  • A17-20
  • B13-16
  • C9-12
  • D5-8
  • E0-4

  • Analysis of how the elements of music are used

    • APrecise, insightful analysis of how elements are manipulated and combined.
    • BClear analysis of several elements and how they combine.
    • CSome analysis; mostly description.
    • DLimited analysis; mainly lists elements.
    • EMinimal analysis.

  • Understanding of style and context

    • AThorough understanding of stylistic, cultural, historical or social context.
    • BGood understanding of context with relevant links.
    • CSome understanding of context.
    • DLimited understanding of context.
    • ELittle or no context.

  • Musical evidence and terminology

    • AUses precise terminology and well-chosen musical examples throughout.
    • BUses correct terminology and relevant examples.
    • CSome terminology and examples; not always accurate.
    • DLimited terminology or examples.
    • ELittle accurate terminology.

  • Presentation and communication (multimodal or viva voce)

    • AClear, well-structured, engaging presentation with confident delivery.
    • BClear, structured presentation.
    • CAdequate presentation; structure uneven.
    • DBasic presentation; unclear in places.
    • EMinimal or unclear presentation.

Self-assessment against the rubric

Find the rubric for your focus area above. For each of the four criteria, decide honestly which band you are closest to right now.

  1. My focus area

    Performing, Composing, or Listening and Investigation?

  2. Where I am strong

    Which criterion and band are you closest to at the top end?

  3. Where I need to improve most

    Which criterion and band are you weakest in, and what would move it up a band?

  4. My plan for the time I have left

    What will you actually do in your remaining sessions?

Your answers stay on this device.

Before you submit

  1. Your product is uploaded to the correct Canvas assignment, or handed to Mr Edwards, by the due date.
  2. Any score, lead sheet, or slides that are part of your product are included, not left on your device.
  3. Instruments, equipment, backing tracks or files you need for your presentation are ready to bring.
  4. If you used generative AI for planning or research, you have noted what you used it for. It has no place in composing, arranging, producing, performing, or writing your spoken introduction.
