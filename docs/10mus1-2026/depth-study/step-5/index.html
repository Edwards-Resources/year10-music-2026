Step 5

Step 5: Present it

Step 5 of 5, worked on in Term 4, Weeks 4 and 5.

Learning intention

I can introduce my project to the class, present it clearly, and give useful feedback as an audience member.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Key word

Spoken introduction

A short, unscripted-sounding explanation you give before your performance, recording, or investigation starts. It tells the class what you set out to do, so they know what to listen or watch for. It is part of your mark, not a formality before the real thing starts.

What a good spoken introduction covers

  1. What you made. One sentence naming your product: the piece, the song, the investigation topic.
  2. Why you chose it. The genuine reason, even if it is simple. "I wanted to get better at improvising" is a real reason.
  3. What to listen or watch for. Point the class at the one thing you are proudest of, so they notice it.

Plan your spoken introduction

Audience feedback

Fill this in while you watch a classmate present, then hand or tell them your two lines afterwards. Clear it and reuse it for the next presentation.

  1. Who presented

    Whose project did you watch?

  2. What I noticed

    What stood out about what they made or how they presented it?

  3. One thing I'd ask them

    A genuine question you'd want to know the answer to.
