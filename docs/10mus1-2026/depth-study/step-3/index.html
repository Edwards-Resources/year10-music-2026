Lesson 3: Develop it

Step 3 of 5, worked on across Term 4, Weeks 1 to 3.

Learning intention

I can work steadily on my project, use feedback to improve it, and keep a process journal that shows my thinking.

Success criteria

Tick them off as you go.

Step by stepWhat a good journal entry has

  1. What you actually did this session, in one line.
  2. One decision you made and the reason for it. "I changed the key because..." is worth more than "I worked on the chorus."
  3. One thing you will do next session, so you do not lose ten minutes remembering where you were.

Key word

Process journal
A short, regular record of your project as you make it: what you worked on, what you changed and why, and what you will do next. It is not a diary of feelings, it is evidence of your thinking, and it is what makes your spoken introduction easy to write later.

Fill this inJournal entry

Fill this in at the end of a work session. It only holds one entry at a time, so copy it somewhere safe (a notes app, a photo, your workbook) before you clear it and write the next one.

  1. Date and session

    Which lesson or session is this?

  2. What I worked on

    What did you actually do this session?

  3. What I changed and why

    Any decision you made and the reason for it.

  4. What’s next

    What will you do in your next session?

Type straight into the boxes. Your answers stay on this device.

Fill this inPeer feedback

Use this with your feedback partner at each progress check (Term 4, Week 2 and Week 3). Swap roles: fill it in for their project, then have them fill it in for yours.

  1. Whose project

    Who are you giving feedback to?

  2. What I saw or heard

    Describe what they showed you, without judging it yet.

  3. One strength

    What is working well?

  4. One next step

    What is the single most useful thing they could do next?

Type straight into the boxes. Your answers stay on this device.

Exit ticket

One sentence: what did this progress check tell you to change?

Depth Study: all 5 steps

Depth Study

Before Term 4: Choosing and proposing

The work that decides how the whole unit goes. You choose a project that you actually care about and can actually finish, then write the plan that gets it approved.

  1. 1Choose your projectI can choose a depth study project I care about and can finish in the time I have.
  2. 2Write your proposalI can write a proposal that sets out my goal, my product, my plan and how I will know it worked.

Term 4, Weeks 1 to 3: Developing it

The longest stretch of the unit. You build your product, get feedback twice, and keep a running record of your thinking so the write-up for your presentation is easy at the end.

  1. 3Develop itI can work steadily on my project, use feedback to improve it, and keep a process journal that shows my thinking.

Term 4, Weeks 3 to 4: Refining and finalising

You compare your work honestly against the rubric for your focus area, fix the highest-impact gaps first, and check your submission is actually ready before the due date.

  1. 4Refine and finaliseI can check my project against the rubric for my focus area, fix the gaps that matter most, and get it ready to submit.

Term 4, Weeks 4 to 5: Presenting it

You submit your product, present it to the class with a short spoken introduction, and then take your turn as an audience member for everyone else’s presentations.

  1. 5Present itI can introduce my project to the class, present it clearly, and give useful feedback as an audience member.
