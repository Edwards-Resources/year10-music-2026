Step 3

Step 3: Develop it

Step 3 of 5, worked on across Term 4, Weeks 1 to 3.

Learning intention

I can work steadily on my project, use feedback to improve it, and keep a process journal that shows my thinking.

Success criteria

tick them off as you go, they save on your device

Key word

Process journal

A short, regular record of your project as you make it: what you worked on, what you changed and why, and what you will do next. It is not a diary of feelings, it is evidence of your thinking, and it is what makes your spoken introduction easy to write later.

What a good journal entry has

  1. What you actually did this session, in one line.
  2. One decision you made and the reason for it. "I changed the key because..." is worth more than "I worked on the chorus."
  3. One thing you will do next session, so you do not lose ten minutes remembering where you were.

Journal entry

Fill this in at the end of a work session. It only holds one entry at a time, so copy it somewhere safe (a notes app, a photo, your workbook) before you clear it and write the next one.

  1. Date and session

    Which lesson or session is this?

  2. What I worked on

    What did you actually do this session?

  3. What I changed and why

    Any decision you made and the reason for it.

  4. What's next

    What will you do in your next session?

Your answers stay on this device.

Peer feedback

Use this with your feedback partner at each progress check (Term 4, Week 2 and Week 3). Swap roles: fill it in for their project, then have them fill it in for yours.

  1. Whose project

    Who are you giving feedback to?

  2. What I saw or heard

    Describe what they showed you, without judging it yet.

  3. One strength

    What is working well?

  4. One next step

    What is the single most useful thing they could do next?

Your answers stay on this device.

Before you leave

One sentence: what did this progress check tell you to change?
