# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML generated by a dependency-free build script from per-lesson data files.

Chosen against the user's stated requirement that this must serve every class, every topic, year after year. That requirement rules out both alternatives offered: hand-written pages drift out of sync across hundreds of lessons, and a framework (Astro, Eleventy) introduces a Node dependency tree that needs periodic maintenance from a user who writes no code and will not be in a session when it breaks. A build script using only the language runtime already on the machine has no lockfile, no install step, and nothing to rot. Deploy target is GitHub Pages, which constrains the answer to something that emits plain static files.

Structured for many courses and many cohort years from the first commit, not retrofitted later.

## Users

**Primary: the Year 10 Music class**, aged about 15, working in their classroom on school devices during 60-minute periods, and at home when catching up after an absence. They arrive at a specific lesson, need to find it without navigating a hierarchy, listen to audio, and write a response in their exercise book. Their attention is on the audio and their book, not on the page.

**Secondary: Matthew, teaching from it live.** He projects the page and works down it with the class. His stated reason for wanting this over Canvas is not having to click in and out of pages and modules mid-lesson to reach a recording or a slide.

Later cohorts and other classes (Year 8 Music, Year 8 Technology Mandatory, Year 12 Music 1, Year 11 Drama) are confirmed future audiences, not speculative ones.

## Product Purpose

A live teaching site that carries the lesson content students work from in class and return to when absent. It replaces the Canvas content pages, not Canvas itself.

Success is Matthew teaching a full lesson from the site without opening anything else, and an absent student getting back in sequence without asking him what they missed.

## Positioning

The site knows where the class actually is.

Canvas pages are fixed to a week: a Week 2 page is always a Week 2 page, even when the class reaches it in Week 3. This site carries both the registered lesson sequence and the class's real position in it, updated from Matthew's daily debriefs. No other tool in his stack can show that, and it is the reason the site exists rather than a feature on top of it.

## Operating Context

- NSW public school, NESA Stage 5 Music. The registered program is the authority; the site presents it and must never contradict it.
- Term structure: 10-week terms, fortnightly A/B timetable cycle, 60-minute periods.
- Lessons slip constantly through cover, carnivals, assemblies and exam blocks. The gap between the registered sequence and the real position is the normal state, not an exception.
- Daily debriefs are the update ritual. Content changes flow from the debrief into the repo, then to the site.
- Canvas remains the system for assessment: task notifications, submissions, SpeedGrader, quizzes and marks. The site links to Canvas for those; it never duplicates them.
- Students reach the site by URL, including from a link in Canvas.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Must carry:** the full registered lesson sequence for a topic, per-lesson learning intentions and success criteria, listening material, class activities, definitions and vocabulary, and a visible marker of where the class is up to.

**Must never carry:** student names, student work, marks, Sentral or markbook data, NESA past papers or marking guidelines, commercial audio or video files. Audio and video are embedded from their host (YouTube), never uploaded.

**Hosting:** GitHub Pages, public repository, publicly reachable with no login. Access-controlled Pages requires GitHub Enterprise Cloud and is not available here. The site carries a noindex tag so it does not appear in search results. Decided 5 August 2026: repos live in a free GitHub organisation under Matthew's existing account, publishing at `<org>.github.io/<site>`, keeping teaching repos separate from his iOS app repos. Org name not yet chosen.

**Scope, decided 5 August 2026: one site per class per year.** A Year 10 Music 2026 site holds all four term topics; each class gets one URL to bookmark for the year, and each new year gets a fresh set of sites (about seven a year). Topics may carry their own accent or sub-theme inside the class site's world. The per-topic alternative (about 28 sites a year) was considered and rejected as unmaintainable.

**School identity:** no school logo, letterhead or brand assets, and (decided 5 August 2026) **no school name anywhere in this repository, even in plain text and even in the internal planning files** - the site is anonymous. A personal public site carrying official school identity reads as an official school publication, which is a separate approval conversation. The site's identity is its own: the crow and the marker voice.

**Not yet resolved:** whether NSW Department of Education policy places conditions on staff publishing teaching material to a personal external host. To be checked before the URL goes to students. Nothing about this has been verified, and it must not be assumed either way.

**Content debt, confirmed:** the Sound Detectives lessons carry 23 listening slots between them, and **none of the 23 has a recording attached.** The remaining lessons (the AT3 exam week and the Depth Study launch week among them) have no listening slot at all by design. Filling the 23 requires Matthew's judgement on repertoire and is tracked as separate work. Corrected 5 August 2026: an earlier version of this line said "23 of the 30 lessons have unfilled placeholders", which wrongly implied seven lessons already had audio.

## Brand Commitments

- Australian English throughout.
- No em dashes in anything student-facing or teacher-facing.
- Fresh visual identity, explicitly approved. No inheritance from the existing Canvas page styling and no school brand assets.
- **Distinctive and engaging, by explicit reversal.** On 5 August 2026 Matthew overturned his earlier "conventional category-standard" choice: he wants the site to "look amazing, interactive and engaging for the kids", a fully functioning premium website rather than a plain school course site. The convention-over-distinctive commitment recorded earlier that day no longer stands. Direction is chosen from concrete mockup options he approves, not assumed.
- **What the earlier craft bar still contributes:** the structural virtues named with BBC Bitesize and Khan Academy survive the reversal as requirements, not as an aesthetic. An unambiguous course to topic to lesson hierarchy, visible position in a sequence, short scannable lesson pages mixing text with embedded media, generous type, and accessibility treated as a requirement all still hold inside whatever distinctive world is chosen.

## Product Principles

1. **The registered program is the spine; the live position is the overlay.** Never let the second quietly rewrite the first, because the program is a compliance document.
2. **A lesson page is used with hands busy.** Students are holding a pen and listening. Anything that needs careful reading to operate has failed.
3. **Public means public.** Every addition is judged on whether it would be safe on a billboard, because functionally it is on one.
4. **Built once for every class.** No decision that only works for Year 10 Music, and no content-specific styling that the next topic cannot inherit.
5. **It has to survive Matthew not being in a session.** No dependency, build step or service that will break unattended.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Students in this cohort have access provisions applied under their learning plans. The site must remain fully usable with browser zoom and text scaling, meet WCAG AA contrast, and not depend on colour alone to convey the class position or lesson status. Listening activities must not assume headphones or a particular device.
